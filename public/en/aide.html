1. First start

The very first time you switch it on, the tablet guides you step by step: choosing the language, connecting, then the NUR assistant for your city, the calculation method and the muezzin. The video below covers the full journey, on the system side as well as in the app.

The language chosen at the very beginning applies to the whole tablet. NUR also has its own language setting, should you want the app to differ from the system.

2. Connecting to Wi-Fi

Wi-Fi is not set up in NUR but in the system bar, which is hidden at the top of the screen. It's the gesture nobody guesses.

  1. Place your finger at the very top of the screen and swipe down.
  2. Press and hold the Wi-Fi tile to open the list of networks.
  3. Choose your network and enter its password.

Long press, not a short tap. A brief tap on the tile only switches Wi-Fi off or on: it's the long press that opens the list of networks.

One confusing detail: the Wi-Fi tile doesn't show the word "Wi-Fi" but the name of the network you are connected to.

Sharing your phone's connection works: switch on the hotspot on the phone, then choose it here like an ordinary Wi-Fi network.

3. Adjusting the sound

One important point: the adhan and the Quran recitations have two separate volumes. Turning one up doesn't affect the other — this is the most common reason for sound that seems low when you thought you had turned it up.

The safest way is to adjust it from NUR. Settings open on five sections, on the left of the screen; the two volumes live in two of them: Adhan for the call to prayer, Reading for the Quran recitations.

The number shown tells you where you stand. "7 / 7" or "15 / 15" means you are already at maximum: the app isn't holding anything back, so beyond that it's the tablet's speaker reaching its limit, and there's no point looking for another setting elsewhere.

4. Listening to the Quran offline

Recitations are downloaded as you listen. Every verse you have already listened to stays on the tablet and then works offline: only what you have never listened to is missing.

To get ahead, you have two options. In the reader, the Options button downloads the current surah. And in Settings, a button downloads the entire Quran.

The full download takes time and a good Wi-Fi connection. You can pause it and start it again: it resumes where it left off, and it carries on even if you leave the app or put the tablet down.

5. Resuming your reading

NUR remembers by itself where you stopped. This position is called Last read and is updated every time you leave the reader — there is nothing for you to set or update yourself.

  1. Open Read the Quran.
  2. Choose the Bookmarks tab.
  3. The first line is your position: tap it to go back there.

The bookmarks you set yourself, with the reader's 🔖 icon, appear below, from newest to oldest, each with the day you set it. A long press on one of them lets you delete it. Setting a bookmark again on a verse already marked moves it to the top of the list, without duplicating it.

Bookmarks set before the update have no date: it wasn't recorded at the time. They remain usable as they are.

You don't have to look for this choice: the first time you have a reading in progress, NUR will ask you once and for all whether you prefer to resume where you stopped, or go back to the surah index. You can change it later, in Settings, Reading section.

6. Memorising

The reader has a learning mode that automatically repeats each verse — 2, 3 or 5 times — before moving on to the next, and gives you time to say it back.

7. Reading comfort

An e-ink screen doesn't light up: it redraws itself. Depending on how it goes about it, scrolling briefly passes through black — which we perceive as a flicker — or glides without showing anything.

Your tablet is set to "fast". It's the most comfortable mode for reading: no flicker, the text scrolls in one go. There's nothing for you to do — if you have installed the latest update, it's already in place.

To check or change it, pull the top bar down:

What the four do:

  • regal — the sharpest image, but a flicker at every page change. Pleasant to look at, tiring for long reading.
  • Default — the tablet's original setting, halfway between. This is what caused the flickering you may have noticed.
  • fast — the one we install. No flicker, at the cost of a faint trace of the previous text, which you forget as you read.
  • Maximum speed — avoid. The screen goes faster still, but it leaves ghosting: the ghosts of previous pages build up and end up getting in the way of reading.

This setting belongs to the tablet, not to NUR: it applies to everything on the screen. If you change it, NUR respects your choice and does not put it back to "fast" at the next start.

8. Prayer times

Prayer times depend on two things: your city and the calculation method. The method recommended for your country is offered at the top of the list — in France, the Paris Mosque. Since version 0.8, the Paris Mosque, Algeria, Tunisia, Morocco, Turkey, Emirates, Indonesia and Malaysia methods follow the calendar published by their authority.

Don't forget Save, at the bottom of the settings screen, after changing city.

9. The adhan

You choose the muezzin from several recitations, and you can listen to it straight away to check the setting and the volume.

Prayer by prayer, you can choose the full adhan, a simple beep, or silence: the small icons to the right of each time, on the main screen, cycle through these three modes.

When the adhan sounds, a screen appears with a Stop the adhan button. The notification at the top of the screen offers the same button.

10. Updating the tablet

NUR checks for updates by itself once a day and offers to install them. You can also check on demand from the settings.

The Updates page details what each version brings: new features, improvements and fixes.

11. Other settings

Settings open on five sections, listed on the left of the screen: Prayer, Adhan, Reading, Device, Help & tools. Each shows its current setting — your city, the chosen muezzin, the language — and you tap one to see its details on the right. The Qibla compass, the update check and this help are in Help & tools; everything that follows is in Device.

Screen rotation is off to begin with. On a tablet sitting in its dock, it costs a dozen hours of battery life for a service that brings nothing. Switch it on if you turn the tablet to read.

Kiosk mode makes NUR the tablet's home screen: the Home button always brings you back to the prayer times. This is the factory setting. Switching it off restores the usual Android home screen.

The advanced settings, below the calculation method, let you adjust the angles, shift a prayer by a few minutes or choose the school for Asr. Only touch them if your mosque follows a particular calculation.

12. The hadith library

The Library button, at the bottom of the home screen, opens the collections: Riyad as-Salihin, Nawawi's 40 hadiths, Sahih al-Bukhari, Sunan Abu Dawud, Sunan Ibn Majah, Sunan an-Nasa'i, the 40 Qudsi hadiths, those of ad-Dahlawi, the HadeethEnc encyclopedia and Hisn al-Muslim. Nearly 28,700 hadiths, all on the tablet, offline.

  1. Tap a collection: its table of contents appears.
  2. Tap a chapter to read it. The Language button, at the top, offers Arabic or French.
  3. The Back button returns to the table of contents, then to the list of collections.

A long chapter, like those of Sahih al-Bukhari, takes a few seconds to appear: that's normal, the tablet prepares it in full.

13. Arabic letters

The Arabic letters button, on the home screen, opens eight steps, in the order of the Quranic school: the letter on its own, then with vowels, then the syllable, then the word, up to the first verses. Each card can be heard in the voice of a native speaker, recorded for NUR.

  1. Open a step, listen to the card, repeat, then say whether you know it.
  2. The day's review brings back the cards to go over. You move on to the next step once the previous one is mastered.
  3. The Articulation button shows, for each letter, where the sound comes from.

A way to get started, a little each day: you become familiar with the letters and their sounds. The tablet does not hear your pronunciation.

14. Text size

Since version 0.8, a single setting enlarges the text across the whole tablet: the home screen, the prayer times, the Quran, the hadiths and the settings themselves.

  1. Open Settings (the cog wheel at the top right of the home screen).
  2. Choose the Device panel, then Text size.
  3. Pick Normal, Large or Extra large. The screen redraws straight away.

In the Quran and the books, the − and + buttons also adjust the reading text size, independently of this general setting.
